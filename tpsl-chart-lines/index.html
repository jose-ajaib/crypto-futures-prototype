Showing unlimited TP/SL orders on the futures chart

Density based clustering instead of a fixed cap. One rule that scales from 2 orders to 50, and degrades predictably instead of hiding things the trader is watching.

BTCIDR-PERP · Futures One way mode No order count limit TradingView Advanced Charts Mobile first Draft for design, eng and PM review

1. The problem, stated precisely

A position can carry any number of TP and SL orders. Each one wants a horizontal line and a label at its price. The failure is not "too many orders", it is too many labels inside too few vertical pixels. The same 6 orders are perfectly readable at one zoom level and an unreadable pile at another, so any solution keyed to order count is solving the wrong variable.

What crowding actually costs

  • Labels overlap, so no price is readable
  • Drag targets collide, so the trader edits the wrong order (real money, no undo)
  • The candles disappear behind chrome, so the chart stops being a chart

Local constraints that shape this

  • IDR prices run to 13 characters (1.650.000.000). Too wide to carry inside a pill, which is why price sits on the scale rather than being truncated
  • The price scale already reserves 74 of 390pt for exactly these numbers, so reusing it costs no new width. It does mean the scale becomes a second lane to arbitrate
  • Touch, so no hover to disambiguate and a 44pt minimum target

2. The core decision

Cluster by pixel proximity, not by count. Group adjacent same side orders whose y positions are closer than one label height (24px), collapse each group into a single chip, and let zoom resolve them. Nothing is ever hidden, only merged, and the merge is undone by a gesture traders already perform constantly.

Why not a maximum of X orders shown

A cap forces you to invent a ranking rule, and every rule silently hides an order somebody was watching. "Where is my stop?" is a trust failure that a crowded chart never causes. A cap also behaves discontinuously: the 6th order appears or vanishes for reasons the trader cannot see, and it stays broken at every zoom level because zoom is not part of the rule.

Collapse the labels, not the lines

Lines are visually cheap and the shape of the ladder is information: four thin lines bunched above price reads instantly as "scaling out here". Chips, prices and drag handles are what create the jank. So keep drawing every line, and let only one label per cluster survive.

3. Three tiers, one rule

These are not three modes to build and toggle. They are what the single clustering rule produces at different densities, which is why it holds at any order count.

TierConditionChart showsEditing
Resolved Every gap ≥ 24px One line per order, a pill carrying side and quantity (TP · 0,01 BTC), and the exact price as a filled badge on the price scale Direct drag on the line
Clustered Some gaps < 24px All lines drawn at 40% opacity, one pill per group (4 TP · 0,02 BTC), a bracket showing the group's price extent, no price badges Tap pill, sheet lists the group with exact prices, drag disabled until resolved
Collapsed All orders of a side inside 24px, or more groups than the lane can hold One blended line at the qty weighted average, one pill per side with the side total, density rail keeps the full picture Sheet only, plus "zoom to separate"

Two things moved out of the pill and each move earns something back. Quantity is now absolute (0,01 BTC) rather than a share of the position, and the price left the pill entirely for the price scale.

Absolute quantity survives a position change, a percentage does not

"20%" is a claim about the position, so it silently re-denominates the moment the trader adds to or partially closes that position: the same label now protects a different amount of BTC. An absolute quantity is a claim about the order itself and stays true. It also makes the number that actually matters computable, which is coverage: sum the side and compare it to the position, and an unprotected remainder becomes a fact you can state ("SL covers 0,03 of 0,05 BTC, 0,02 unprotected") instead of a subtraction the trader has to notice is missing from 100%. The sheet surfaces this and turns amber when a side does not fully cover.

The price scale is already a price-to-pixel lane, so stop duplicating it

An IDR perp price is 13 characters (1.650.000.000). Inside a pill that forced a short form (1,650 M), which loses the last six digits exactly where tick precision matters. The price scale is sized for full prices by definition, and the existing position line already puts its price there as a filled badge. Reusing that lane removes the truncation, and shrinks the pill to side plus quantity, which buys back roughly 40% of its width in the most contested part of the plot.

4. Prototype

Built on the current BTCIDR-PERP chart tab. The scenario is a long with 6 TP and 3 SL orders, which is a realistic scale out ladder and already enough to break a naive implementation. There are two builds below: 4a is the full proposal, 4b is the same clustering with off screen handling removed, for scoping the first release.

  • Drag the chart to pan. Horizontally it scrolls through time and the order lines stay pinned. Vertically it changes which orders are on screen, so clusters re-form and edge chips update live.
  • Drag the zoom slider to move through all three tiers.
  • Tap a grouped pill (4 TP · 0,02 BTC) to open the sheet, which lists exact prices and the side's coverage of the position, then use Pisahkan to zoom until the group resolves.
  • Watch the price scale. Resolved orders put a filled badge there at full precision and the tick label underneath is suppressed. Zoom in until an order badge has to yield to the liquidation or mark badge and its price falls back into the pill.
  • Return to centre with the "terkini" control that appears on the time axis once you have panned away.

4b. Reduced scope: clustering only

Same clustering rule, same pills, same price scale badges. What is removed is everything that accounts for orders outside the viewport: the edge chips at the top and bottom of the plot, and the density rail. Both are answers to "is there something I cannot see", which is a different question from "these labels are on top of each other".

The two prototypes share one scenario and one zoom range, so put them side by side and drive them to the same state. The instructive move is the vertical pan. Drag an SL below the bottom of the plot in each. The full version leaves a chip that keeps the order in the count; this one lets it leave without a trace, and the position looks less protected than it is. That is the actual cost of the cut, and it is a cost that only shows up when the trader is zoomed in, which is also when they are most likely to be managing risk.

What survives the cut, and why that makes it shippable

Clustering is self contained: it reads the y positions it already has and merges them. It needs no new state, no knowledge of the full order book, and no second render pass. Off screen accounting needs the opposite, an awareness of orders the chart is not drawing, which is where the TradingView coordinate work and the rAF dirty check are spent. So the cut is along a real seam rather than a convenience. Ship 4b, then add edge chips and the rail as a follow up without touching the clustering code.

The one case where 4b is not merely reduced but wrong

The liquidation line. When it pans off screen, the reduced version removes the trader's only on chart reference for how much room is left, and unlike a TP that is a safety signal rather than a convenience. If 4b ships first, the anchors (position, liquidation, mark) should keep an edge chip even though the orders do not. That is a fixed number of chips, three at most, so it costs none of the density logic being deferred.

13 digit prices are why the price scale does the work

A full IDR perp price (1.650.000.000) does not fit a pill on a 390pt screen. Rather than truncate it to 1,650 M and lose the last six digits, the price moves to the price scale, which is already sized for exactly this string. The local number format still shapes the pattern here, it just pushes the price into a different lane instead of shortening it.

5. Interaction rules

Extending the existing position line, not inventing a label

The live position line already solves both halves of this: a segmented pill carrying the quantity in BTC and a running PnL, and the exact price as a filled badge on the price scale. Order labels inherit that anatomy rather than competing with it, so the only thing a trader has to learn is that green and red pills mean orders while blue means the position.

The segment count stays fixed and only the trailing segment changes meaning, which is what makes the collapse legible. A resolved order reads side then quantity. A group reads count and side, then the summed quantity, then a chevron where the position line has its close action. Same shape, so the trader reads "there is more behind this" without a legend, and an action slot never turns into a disclosure slot in place.

Label lanes

Position anchors (mark, position, liquidation) live in the left lane and never collapse, because they are what a trader reads position state from. Orders live in the right lane against the price scale. Two lanes means the two categories can never collide, which removes most overlap before clustering runs. The lane split is also forced by cardinality: there is exactly one position line in one way mode, so it can afford the left lane's generous three segment pill, while orders are unbounded and need the lane that clustering governs.

The price scale is a third lane, and it is contested

Moving price onto the scale moves the collision there too, so that lane needs its own rule. It is shared by the tick labels, the liquidation badge, the mark badge, the position badge and now the orders, and unlike a pill a badge cannot be nudged: its entire job is the y to price correspondence, so a badge shifted 20px to avoid an overlap is not offset, it is wrong.

So the rule is priority and suppression, not displacement. Liquidation outranks mark, which outranks the position, which outranks orders; each badge claims a 15px band and a badge that cannot claim one is not drawn. Two consequences follow. Only resolved orders are eligible at all, because a cluster has no single price to put there. And a resolved order whose badge loses its band falls back to carrying the price as an extra pill segment, so the price degrades to a worse position rather than disappearing. In the sweep below, at most two order badges ever had to yield.

Tick labels are the cheapest occupant and simply hide when a badge lands within 11px, since the badge states a price more precisely than the tick it covers.

Panning

The two axes are not symmetrical, and treating them the same is the mistake to avoid. Horizontal panning is pure time travel: order lines are price anchored, span the full width and must not move, so the overlay needs no recalculation at all. Vertical panning is the expensive one, because it changes the price to pixel mapping, which changes cluster membership, which changes what is on screen. Every off screen order becomes an edge chip the moment it leaves the viewport, which is what makes vertical panning feel safe rather than lossy.

Two consequences for the build. First, panning must never grab an order: the chart owns the whole surface, chips own their own hit area, and picking up a line requires a long press. Second, once the trader has panned away from mark price they need one tap back, so a return control appears on the time axis. In production that should follow chart convention and split into two, auto scale on the price axis and scroll to latest on the time axis, rather than one combined reset.

Panning also promotes the density rail from a nice to have into load bearing UI. With a fixed price domain and a viewport window drawn on it, the rail becomes a vertical scroll indicator: it shows where you are in the ladder, how much of it is above or below the fold, and that nothing has been dropped. It is what lets you pan aggressively without losing the plot.

Pill de-overlap

Clustering alone is not sufficient, and testing the logic proved it. TP and SL must never merge into one pill because the merge would be semantically wrong, so a TP cluster sitting just above price and an SL cluster just below can still collide. The fix is two bounded passes: lines stay at their true price, pills get pushed apart to the 24px minimum and clamped inside the lane, and a short leader line owns the difference. Unlike a price badge a pill can be nudged, because its meaning is "this group" rather than "this exact y".

Lane capacity is a second collapse trigger

Proximity clustering has a blind spot worth stating, because it only merges adjacent same side orders. Sides that interleave never merge, so a tight band of alternating TP and SL can produce more pills than the lane physically holds, and the de-overlap pass then has to space them below one chip height, which means they overlap after all. The lane holds 11 pills at 24px, so exceeding that count overrules proximity and collapses each side to one pill. Sixty orders inside a 0,05% band now produce two pills with every quantity still accounted for, where the proximity rule alone produced sixty.

Weight encoding

Line opacity and thickness scale with the order's share of the position, so a 0,02 BTC SL reads heavier than a 0,005 BTC one. This answers "which of these matters" at exactly the moment labels are unavailable. Note that the encoding stays proportional even though the label is now absolute: thickness is comparative by nature, and comparison is the one thing a percentage was genuinely good at.

Drag safety

  • A collapsed line has no single price, so dragging it is ambiguous. Drag is disabled on cluster chips. Expand first, then drag one resolved line.
  • Hit area is 44pt tall even though the line is 1px, and only one handle can be active at a time.
  • On drag, show a magnified callout with the live price, distance % and estimated PnL, and enforce a minimum one tick gap on drop.
  • Long press to pick up (not tap and slide), so chart panning stays the default gesture. Panning must never move an order.

Off screen orders

Edge chips (↓ 2 SL · 0,03 BTC) plus the density rail keep the whole ladder accounted for when the viewport only holds part of it. One chip per side per edge, never one merged chip: pan far enough and both TP and SL can end up above the fold at once, and a single chip would have to lie about the side. Absolute quantities make these chips additive with the on screen pills, so the totals reconcile by inspection, which is the arithmetic a percentage share quietly prevented.

This is the part that is separable, and prototype 4b shows what the product looks like without it. Clustering answers "these labels overlap"; off screen accounting answers "is there something I cannot see". Both are real, only the first is caused by density, and only the first has to ship for the chart to stop breaking. The one exception is the liquidation line, which should keep an edge chip in any release, since a trader losing sight of it is a safety problem rather than a legibility one.

The invariant the whole pattern rests on was checked rather than eyeballed: across 2.673 combinations of zoom range and vertical pan position, every quantity is accounted for in exactly one pill or edge chip, no two pills sit closer than 24px, no two price badges share a 15px band, and no pill escapes the lane.

6. Pseudocode

Language agnostic, and deliberately independent of the charting library. The only thing it needs from the chart is a price to y mapping and a redraw signal, which keeps it testable in isolation and portable if the chart layer changes.

// Runs on every redraw: visible range changed, order list changed, pane resized.
// Deliberately knows nothing about TradingView beyond yOf() and a redraw signal.

STEP 1: SETUP AND BAIL OUT
  scale = chart.pane(0).mainPriceScale()
  IF scale == null OR scale.mode() != REGULAR:          // log / percent / indexed
      hideChips(); showListOnlyAffordance(); RETURN
  { from, to } = scale.getVisiblePriceRange()
  H            = chart.pane(0).getHeight()
  yOf(price)   = (to - price) / (to - from) * H            // linear only

STEP 2: PARTITION VISIBLE VS OFF SCREEN
  visible = [], offAbove = [], offBelow = []
  FOR order IN orders:
      y = yOf(order.price)
      IF      y < EDGE_PAD      -> offAbove.push(order)
      ELSE IF y > H - EDGE_PAD  -> offBelow.push(order)
      ELSE                       -> visible.push({ order, y })

STEP 3: CLUSTER BY PIXEL PROXIMITY    // the whole idea, 8 lines
  sort visible by y ascending
  clusters = []
  FOR v IN visible:
      last = clusters.last()
      IF last exists
         AND last.side == v.order.side                      // never mix TP with SL
         AND (v.y - last.lastY) < THRESHOLD_PX:            // 24px = chip height
          last.add(v)
      ELSE
          clusters.push(newCluster(v))

STEP 3B: CAPACITY OVERRIDE   // proximity only merges ADJACENT SAME SIDE orders, so
                              // interleaved sides can outrun the lane. LANE_CAP = 11
  LANE_CAP = floor((H - 2*LANE_INSET) / THRESHOLD_PX) + 1
  IF clusters.length > LANE_CAP:
      clusters = [ oneClusterPerSide(visible) ]                     // straight to collapsed

STEP 4: RENDER LINES AND QUEUE LABELS
  badges = []                                // price scale lane, priority ordered
  badges.push({ y: yOf(liq),  prio: 0 })     // liquidation outranks everything
  badges.push({ y: yOf(mark), prio: 1 })
  badges.push({ y: yOf(entry), prio: 2 })    // the existing position line badge

  FOR c IN clusters:
      FOR v IN c.items:                                    // always draw every line
          drawLine(y       = v.y,
                   color   = c.side == TP ? green : red,
                   opacity = c.size > 1 ? 0.4 : 1.0,
                   weight  = lerp(1, 3, v.order.qty / positionQty))  // weight = conviction
      IF c.size == 1:
          // pill = [side][qty]. price goes on the scale, NOT in the pill.
          pill = { y: c.items[0].y, segs: [c.side, fmtQty(c.qty)], draggable: true }
          pill.badge = { y: c.items[0].y, prio: 3, price: c.items[0].order.price }
          badges.push(pill.badge)            // eligible only because it is resolved
      ELSE:
          drawBracket(c.firstY, c.lastY)                            // group extent
          // same 3 segment shape as the position line, trailing slot becomes disclosure
          pill = { y: c.midY, tappable: true,
                   segs: [c.size + " " + c.side, fmtQty(c.totalQty), "›"] }
      pills.push(pill)

STEP 4B: PRICE SCALE LANE   // a badge CANNOT be nudged: its whole job is the y to
                            // price correspondence, so it is drawn or it is not drawn
  sort badges by (prio, y)
  claimed = []
  FOR b IN badges:
      IF b.y inside lane AND no c IN claimed WHERE abs(c - b.y) < BAND_PX:
          claimed.push(b.y); b.drawn = true
  drawTickLabels(skipping any within 11px of a drawn badge)         // badge wins
  FOR b IN badges WHERE b.drawn: drawFilledBadge(b.y, fmtPrice(b.price))

STEP 4C: PILL DE-OVERLAP   // unlike a badge, a pill MAY move: it means "this group",
                            // not "this exact y". TP and SL never merge, so they collide
  sort pills by y
  forward pass:   p.cy = max(p.y, previous.cy + THRESHOLD_PX)       // enforces top edge
  backward pass:  p.cy = min(p.cy, next.cy - THRESHOLD_PX)          // enforces bottom edge
  IF pills[0].cy < laneTop:                                        // cannot fit at all
      spread evenly across the lane                                // degrade uniformly, never overlap
  FOR p IN pills:
      IF abs(p.cy - p.y) > 3:
          drawLeaderLine(p.y, p.cy)          // the line stays true, only the label moves
      IF p.badge AND NOT p.badge.drawn:
          p.segs.insert(fmtPrice(p.badge.price))   // fallback, price never vanishes
      drawPill(p.cy, p.segs)

  drawAnchorPills(mark, position, liquidation)  // left lane, exempt from clustering
  IF offAbove: drawEdgeChip(TOP, offAbove)      // one per side per edge, qty in BTC
  IF offBelow: drawEdgeChip(BOTTOM, offBelow)
  drawDensityRail(orders, fixedDomain, viewportWindow)

STEP 5: INPUT
  ON tap(chip) WHERE chip.cluster.size > 1:
      openSheet(chip.cluster.items)                                 // + "zoom to separate"
  ON longPress(chip) WHERE chip.cluster.size == 1:
      beginDrag(chip.order)                                         // 44pt hit area
  ON drag(order, y):
      price = snapToTick(priceOf(y))
      price = clampToValidSide(price, order.side, markPrice)
      IF withinOneTickOfSibling(price): block, show hint
      showMagnifiedCallout(price, distancePct, estimatedPnl)
  ON release: confirmModify(order, price)

INVARIANTS
  1. no order is ever hidden, only merged
  2. a merged line is never draggable                    // ambiguous price = real money risk
  3. anchors never cluster
  4. recompute on RANGE change, not on price TICK        // otherwise labels reflow every frame
  5. THRESHOLD_PX derives from the chip height token, not a magic number
  6. Advanced Charts fires no event for vertical scale change, so poll
     getVisiblePriceRange() + getHeight() on rAF and bail when unchanged
  7. the line always marks the true price, the chip is allowed to move,
     and a leader line owns the difference
  8. horizontal pan invalidates nothing: lines are price anchored and span
     the full width. only vertical pan, pinch and zoom invalidate the layout,
     so gate the whole redraw on a change in { from, to, H }
  9. quantity is absolute, never a share of the position, so a label cannot
     be silently re-denominated by an add or a partial close
 10. a price badge is drawn at its true y or not drawn at all. a pill may be
     nudged. never the reverse
 11. the price scale badge must be fully opaque, because over a real
     TradingView scale we cannot hide the library's own tick labels

7. TradingView Advanced Charts: what is and is not available

This is the part to validate with engineering before committing to the pattern. Verified against the current Advanced Charts docs.

CapabilityStatusDetail
Native order linesNot available createOrderLine() and createPositionLine() became Trading Platform only from v29. On Advanced Charts they cannot be used, so the built in drag, cancel button and quantity label do not exist for us.
Price to pixelNot available No public priceToCoordinate in Advanced Charts. We compute y ourselves from IPriceScaleApi.getVisiblePriceRange() and IPaneApi.getHeight().
Visible price rangeAvailable activeChart().getPanes()[0].getMainSourcePriceScale().getVisiblePriceRange() returns {from, to}. Returns null when the main source is in "No Scale" mode, so guard it.
Scale modeGuard needed IPriceScaleApi.getMode() exposes log / percent / indexed, and isInverted(). Our linear mapping is only correct in regular mode. Either implement the log mapping or hide order chips and fall back to the sheet in non linear modes.
Redraw signalRisk onVisibleRangeChanged(), crossHairMoved() and widget events reset_scales, panes_height_changed, mouse_down / mouse_up exist, but no event fires for vertical price scale change. Vertical pan, pinch and price axis drag therefore need a requestAnimationFrame loop that dirty checks {from, to} and pane height, and returns early when unchanged. Horizontal pan is free because it cannot move a price anchored line. This is the main perf item to prove in the spike.
Drawing shapesFallback only createShape({price},{shape:'horizontal_line'}) with lock, disableSelection, disableSave, disableUndo, zOrder. Fine for static lines, but shapes are drawings: they cannot carry our chip UI or clustering, and cost per shape is undocumented. Use for lines only, or not at all.
Overlay hostConstraint The library renders inside an iframe, so our layer is a sibling absolutely positioned over it. It needs pointer-events:none on the container and auto only on chips and handles, otherwise we swallow pan and zoom.
Price scale badgesConstraint We cannot render into the price scale, because it lives inside the iframe. Our badge is drawn on top of it, which has two consequences. It must be fully opaque, since we cannot selectively hide the library's own tick labels and can only occlude them. And we cannot query the scale's width, so the badge is positioned from the right edge against a configured constant. Worth confirming with engineering that the IDR scale width is stable, since a scale that auto widens when digit count changes would shift our badges. IDR perp prices are a constant 13 characters in the tradeable range, so this is likely safe but should be asserted rather than assumed.

The honest summary for PM

The pattern is buildable on Advanced Charts, but nothing about order lines comes for free. We own the overlay, the coordinate math, the drag behaviour and the redraw loop. The upside of owning it is that clustering, the price scale badge lane and cluster sheets are all impossible with native order lines anyway, so we would be writing this layer even on Trading Platform. One point in our favour: because the position line already ships this exact anatomy, the pill and badge components exist and this is an extension rather than a new pattern. Recommend a one week spike on the redraw loop and vertical pinch before we commit to the interaction.

8. Edge cases to design for

  • Partial fill mid cluster. The chip count changing under the trader's finger is the ugliest case here. Animate the count, keep the sheet open, never reorder rows while a row is being touched.
  • Price crossing the ladder. With price moving through a tight cluster every tick, labels must not reflow on each frame. Recompute clusters on range change, not on price tick, and let the mark line move freely through them.
  • Low volatility pair, tight range. Worst case is many orders inside a narrow band, not many orders overall. Test with 20 orders inside 0,5%, and with sides interleaved rather than separated, which is the case that exposes the lane capacity limit.
  • One order. The pattern must not look like a system at n=1. A single TP shows one plain line, one pill and one price badge, no bracket, no disclosure affordance.
  • Log or percent scale. Pills and badges hide and the sheet becomes the source of truth until the mapping is implemented.
  • Order rejected or cancelled server side. The line must disappear with a reason, not just vanish from the overlay.
  • Order quantity below display precision. A 0,0001 BTC order rounds to 0 at three decimals, so the pill would read "0 BTC" and look like a bug. Either raise precision for small quantities or show a "<0,001" form, and decide which before the first altcoin perp with a much smaller tick.
  • Orders that do not cover the position. Absolute quantities make an unprotected remainder visible, which is new information the percentage view hid. Decide whether that is a passive readout in the sheet or an active warning on the chart, because it is arguably the most valuable thing this change surfaces.
  • Liquidation price inside a cluster. The liquidation badge outranks order badges on the scale, so a stop sitting within 15px of liquidation loses its badge and shows the price in its pill instead. Verify with engineering that this is the priority traders expect, since the alternative reading is that a stop near liquidation is the single most important price on the screen.

9. Open questions

  • Do we need the blended average line in the collapsed tier, or is the total chip enough? Worth a quick usability test with 5 active futures traders.
  • Should the density rail be persistent or only appear while a cluster exists?
  • Is 24px the right threshold on our type scale, or should it be derived from the chip height token so it survives a typography change?
  • Does the trading team want cluster level actions (cancel all TP in this group) or is per order enough for v1?
  • Should a resolved order pill carry PnL as a third segment, the way the position line does? It is the strongest argument for the shared anatomy, but it is also the widest thing we could put in the most contested lane. Leaning no for v1.
  • Does the position line's inline close action have an analogue on order pills? Cancelling a stop by tapping the chart removes protection, which is a worse failure than closing a position by mistake, so v1 keeps cancel in the sheet only. Confirm with the trading team.
  • Should quantity be shown in BTC or in the quote currency? BTC matches the position line and is unit stable, but a trader sizing in rupiah may reason in IDR. A toggle is the obvious answer and the obvious way to add a setting nobody uses.
